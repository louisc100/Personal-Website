# Volatility Harvesting Stock Research

Prepared: April 27, 2026

This is educational research, not personal investment advice. Volatility harvesting works best when you rebalance a basket of volatile, liquid assets with durable narratives rather than trying to guess one perfect stock. With single names, the main danger is permanent impairment: a stock can stay volatile while still trending badly for reasons that do not mean-revert.

## What I screened for

- High liquidity
- Large and recurring price swings
- Clear business or macro catalysts over the next 1 to 4 quarters
- Enough business quality that buying dips is not purely a hope trade

## Bottom line

For a volatility-harvesting style approach, the strongest current candidates are:

1. `AMD`
2. `NVDA`
3. `PLTR`
4. `TSLA`

Higher-risk trading satellites:

5. `COIN`
6. `MSTR`

If the goal is steady harvesting rather than adrenaline, I would build around `AMD`, `NVDA`, and `PLTR`, then size `TSLA` smaller. I would treat `COIN` and especially `MSTR` as tactical satellites only.

## Stock-by-stock view

### 1) AMD

Why it stands out:

- AMD reported record Q4 2025 revenue of $10.3 billion, with strong full-year results, which gives it a real fundamental base instead of pure story momentum.
- AMD is due to report Q1 2026 results on **May 5, 2026**, which gives the stock a near-term catalyst.
- As of **April 24, 2026**, AMD had surged enough to top a **$500 billion** market cap for the first time, showing both strength and the kind of momentum-driven overshoots that create rebalance opportunities.

Why it fits volatility harvesting:

- Big AI narrative
- Violent re-ratings around product cycles and earnings
- Deep liquidity
- Still tied to tangible demand in CPUs and accelerators

Main risk:

- After a huge April run, valuation risk is real. This is promising, but not cheap.

Verdict:

`Best mix of momentum and business quality right now.`

### 2) NVDA

Why it stands out:

- NVIDIA reported fiscal Q4 2026 revenue of **$68.1 billion**, up **73% year over year**, with data center revenue of **$62.3 billion**.
- On **April 24, 2026**, NVDA closed around **$208.26**, its first record close since October 2025.

Why it fits volatility harvesting:

- Possibly the cleanest long-term AI leader in the group
- Extremely liquid
- Frequent sentiment swings despite strong fundamentals
- Easier to average into than lower-quality story stocks

Main risk:

- It may be too strong and too institutionally owned to be the most explosive harvester. You may get smoother swings than with TSLA or COIN.

Verdict:

`Best core holding for a volatility basket, even if not the wildest one.`

### 3) PLTR

Why it stands out:

- Palantir is scheduled to report Q1 2026 results on **May 4, 2026**.
- Its most recent reported quarter was very strong: Q4 2025 revenue reached roughly **$1.41 billion**, up about **70% year over year**, with especially strong U.S. commercial and government growth.

Why it fits volatility harvesting:

- Strong AI revenue narrative
- Regular sharp swings on sentiment and valuation debates
- Still supported by real contract wins and operating leverage

Main risk:

- Valuation can compress hard if growth slows even a little.

Verdict:

`Very good harvesting candidate if you can tolerate software multiple risk.`

### 4) TSLA

Why it stands out:

- Tesla released Q1 2026 financial results on **April 22, 2026**.
- Recent reporting around that release showed revenue near **$22.4 billion** and a market narrative increasingly centered on robotaxi, AI, and robotics rather than just autos.
- TSLA closed at **$387.51** on April 22 ahead of earnings, and it remains one of the most actively traded large-cap stocks in the market.

Why it fits volatility harvesting:

- Huge liquidity
- Repeated narrative-driven overreactions
- Strong retail and institutional participation
- Plenty of catalysts

Main risk:

- Tesla can trend on narrative for longer than expected in either direction. It is great for volatility, but not always great for predictability.

Verdict:

`Excellent for active harvesting, but size it smaller than the semis.`

### 5) COIN

Why it stands out:

- Coinbase is set to report Q1 2026 results on **May 7, 2026**.
- COIN remains tightly linked to crypto prices, trading volumes, stablecoin activity, and U.S. regulatory developments.
- Around **April 24, 2026**, COIN was trading roughly around **$214 to $215** in quoted options-market snapshots, while Bitcoin itself was around **$77,000 to $78,000**.

Why it fits volatility harvesting:

- Very high realized volatility
- Clear link to crypto cycle
- Often snaps back violently after oversold periods

Main risk:

- You are effectively making a second-order crypto bet. If Bitcoin and alt activity cool off, COIN can underperform quickly.

Verdict:

`Good tactical satellite, not my favorite core holding.`

### 6) MSTR

Why it stands out:

## Suggested basket structure

If I were building this specifically for volatility harvesting, I would think in tiers:

- Core: `NVDA`, `AMD`, `PLTR`
- Smaller active sleeve: `TSLA`
- Tactical only: `COIN`
- Speculative micro-sleeve only: `MSTR`

One simple version:

- 30% `NVDA`
- 25% `AMD`
- 20% `PLTR`
- 15% `TSLA`
- 7% `COIN`
- 3% `MSTR`

That is not a recommendation to buy those exact weights. It is just a way to express the idea that the most violent names should usually be the smallest ones.

## How I would actually harvest volatility

- Rebalance on fixed bands, not feelings. Example: trim when a name rises 20% to 25% above target weight and add when it falls 20% to 25% below.
- Do not harvest a stock through an earnings event unless you already accept gap risk.
- Keep cash available. Volatility harvesting fails when you are fully deployed and cannot add on sharp down moves.
- Use a basket, not one name. The strategy works better when winners fund laggards.
- Review correlation. `COIN` and `MSTR` are both crypto-sensitive, so they can move together in ugly ways.
